= Our logo shouldn't need a proprietary player
:description: Why DevCentr's new mark stays in SVG—and why we're cautious about Rive as a default for open brands.
:revdate: 2026-07-24
:keywords: news, blog, brand, svg, lottie, rive, popkorn, motion

We finally gave DevCentr a proper mark: an orbital hub that reads as a center of gravity. You'll see it on the site, in the GitHub avatar, and in the motion loop that doesn't need JavaScript to breathe.

That last part matters more than it sounds.

There's a loud fashion in product design right now: ship brand motion as a black-box file, drop in a player, call it modern. Rive is genuinely good at interactive chrome—state machines, theme hooks, the works. Its runtimes are open. The *editor* isn't, and the `.riv` format is binary. That means weak git diffs and a hard wall for anyone (human or agent) who wants to edit the motion as text.

We're building an open ecosystem. Our logo shouldn't be the one asset that only opens in a paid app.

So the default for portable marks stays animated SVG and CSS. Lottie when you've got a baked After Effects timeline. Rive when you're already deep in interactive app UI and the player is justified. We're documenting those lanes in General Knowledge; the short version is: **don't hard-advocate a proprietary editor as the open standard for brand motion.**

Longer term we want something text-first that agents can actually edit. link:https://github.com/ayarse/popkorn[Popkorn] is an early CSS-shaped candidate—interesting, not a Dev-Centr default yet. If that future interests you, there's an open thread on rich interactivity at link:https://github.com/ayarse/popkorn/issues/7[ayarse/popkorn#7].

The mark is the visible change. The opinion underneath it is the story: open brands should travel as open source.
